# Direction 02 Landing Page — PM and Design Analysis

This folder expands the Living Business Network homepage into a realistic desktop scroll sequence. It preserves the direction's activity-led premise while keeping Directory and Recruitment as the stable product foundation.

## Current concept audit

### Product-manager perspective

#### What already works

- The proposition, “Discover the businesses behind the work,” is differentiated and understandable.
- Universal search and the two product gateways remain available before the activity content.
- Company work, reviews, and hiring show multiple reasons to revisit the platform.
- Public activity creates a natural connection between company discovery and recruitment.
- The concept avoids reactions, followers, reposts, and other social-network mechanics.

#### Product problems in the original single-screen concept

- A single workplace image visually anchors updates from three different companies, which can falsely imply that the image evidences every update.
- The sponsor occupies premium space beside organic activity before the platform has established trust.
- The activity set has no visible time window, selection logic, topic control, or moderation explanation.
- “Summary” is ambiguous: it is not clear whether it is AI-generated, editorially selected, or a simple count.
- Stable company and job records are compressed into small lower modules, making the product appear closer to a news feed than a Directory and Recruitment platform.
- The page does not explicitly explain that applications, inquiries, and private communication never appear in public activity.

#### Revised product sequence

The landing page should answer four questions:

1. **What is Bharat Navya, and where can I go?** Search, Directory, or Recruitment.
2. **What are credible businesses doing now?** Explore a finite, moderated activity window with clear attribution.
3. **Where are the stable records behind the updates?** Open company profiles and current roles rather than staying in a feed.
4. **How do guidance, sponsorship, privacy, and trust work?** Read useful guides, understand paid placement, and choose a final route.

#### Primary product outcomes

- Search started.
- Directory or Recruitment gateway selected.
- Public update opened into its source company, review, work evidence, or job record.
- Company profile viewed after activity discovery.
- Role viewed or saved after hiring activity discovery.
- Guide opened in the context of a real decision.
- Sponsored engagement measured separately from organic activity.

### Designer perspective

#### What already works

- Plum Ink, Iris, Coral, Aqua, Lavender Mist, and white give the direction a recognisable and more energetic identity.
- The large activity-stage image is a strong signature candidate.
- Sora-like display typography and colour-coded activity types create a contemporary rhythm.
- Photography adds human and operational context.
- Organic company updates, reviews, and roles have visibly different icon treatments.

#### Design problems in the original single-screen concept

- The centred hero is visually generic compared with the more distinctive activity section below it.
- The activity stage, sponsor rail, top companies, roles, and guide all compete within one viewport.
- The sponsor's height and warm surface give it comparable emphasis to the core activity stage.
- A shared image paired with unrelated activity items weakens visual truth.
- Lower cards are too compressed and lack a clear bridge from activity to stable records.
- The page has colour variety but insufficient vertical rhythm and section-level whitespace.

#### Revised design principle

Spend the direction's boldness on one component: the **current activity stage**. One selected, fully attributed public update owns the large image. Adjacent activity items act as selectors and use their own thumbnails or lettermarks. Selecting another item changes the stage rather than implying shared evidence.

Everything after the stage becomes calmer and record-led. This prevents the direction from drifting into a social feed.

## Desktop scroll sequence

### Screen 01 — Landing and live-platform preview

- Header, concise proposition, universal search, and equal Directory/Recruitment gateways.
- A compact “Active this week” preview showing bounded counts by update type.
- The beginning of the activity stage visible at the lower edge.
- No sponsor in the first viewport.

### Screen 02 — Current business activity

- Finite seven-day activity window with filters for All, Company work, Reviews, and Hiring.
- One selected update with its own large work image, company identity, date, structured facts, and source action.
- Adjacent selectors for other updates, each with independent attribution.
- Clear moderation and privacy copy.
- No reactions, follower counts, reposts, or endless feed.

### Screen 03 — Records behind the activity

- Organic company profiles connected to the selected activity.
- Current roles connected to hiring updates.
- Company cards retain capabilities, location, verification, review context, and profile actions.
- Role cards retain employer, location, workplace, salary, freshness, and role actions.
- A clear explanation that activity assists discovery but profiles and role pages remain authoritative.

### Screen 04 — Guidance, sponsorship, and close

- Practical guides tied to company evaluation, reviews, inquiries, and applications.
- A clearly separated sponsor placement without update styling, verification, or organic prominence.
- Public/private activity explanation and reporting controls.
- Final Directory and Recruitment calls to action.
- Complete footer.

## Interaction and motion notes

- The activity stage changes only after explicit selection; it does not auto-rotate while the user is reading.
- One restrained crossfade may transition the selected image and facts. Reduced-motion mode changes content instantly.
- Topic chips filter the finite activity window and always show the active seven-day scope.
- Every activity item links to a stable company, review, work-evidence, or role record.
- AI summaries are labelled, cite their source items, and never merge unrelated company evidence.
- Private inquiries, applications, saved records, and messages never appear publicly.
